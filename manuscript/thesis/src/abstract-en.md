# ABSTRACT

Generative image tools are increasingly used in conceptual product design, yet designers' intentions usually enter prompts as adjectives, the layout, parts and configuration of the outputs are hard to check, and human revisions lack traceable bounds. Taking the conceptual form design of multirotor UAVs as its object, this thesis studies a human–AI collaborative method for parametric controllable generation.

First, evidence cards are compiled from user reviews of consumer aerial-photography drones, and four bipolar word pairs, compact–expansive, simple–complex, unified–assembled and low-key–eye-catching, are mapped to features visible in an orthographic top view: body planform, arm-root transition, nose camera housing and top-cover partition. Second, a form specification card distinguishes target regions, variable items, held items and free items; configuration parameters are expressed by two-dimensional condition images and form parameters by level-wise compiled phrases. Third, the designer screens every draft, selects a parent image and revises it within a fixed number of attempts that can be stopped or rolled back; when revision fails, generation stops and the designer takes over by hand, and the full version chain is kept.

In the main case of a daily-carry aerial-photography quadcopter, the ratio of lateral to longitudinal motor span and the ratio of propeller diameter to diagonal wheelbase both changed monotonically with the input levels under a two-blade part condition image, while text carrying the same information did not. Text-only edits starting from the parent image preserved the non-target parts in five of six images, whereas regeneration from the changed levels altered the whole aircraft. The three-dimensional orientation of the camera in a top view proved to be a boundary of the method: none of three generative revisions reached the target, and the camera was finished by hand. The design case went through three rounds of drafts and three revisions with 43 generation calls. In an agricultural spraying hexacopter case, the layout constraint transferred to the six-rotor configuration but its response contracted towards the regular hexagon, and payload parts had to be checked together with the layout for overlap in the top view. The study shows that writing design intentions as editable form levels, with the designer in control of revision and stopping, keeps generated layouts and key part relations checkable and traceable.

Keywords: UAV form design, parametric design, controllable image generation, human–AI collaboration, design semantics, ControlNet
