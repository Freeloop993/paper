# ABSTRACT

Generative image tools are increasingly used in conceptual product design, yet designers' intentions usually enter prompts as adjectives, the layout, parts and configuration of the outputs are hard to check, and human revisions lack traceable bounds. Taking the conceptual form design of multirotor UAVs as its object, this thesis studies a human–AI collaborative method for parametric controllable generation.

First, evidence cards are compiled from user reviews of consumer aerial-photography drones, and bipolar word pairs such as compact–expansive, simple–complex and unified–assembled are mapped to features visible in an orthographic top view: body planform, arm-root transition, nose protrusion and top-cover partition. Second, a form specification card distinguishes target regions, variable items, held items and free items; configuration parameters are expressed by two-dimensional condition images and form parameters by level-wise compiled phrases. Third, in a prototype configurator the designer selects levels, screens candidates and revises a chosen parent image by changing levels, at most twice, with a complete version chain recorded. Finally, the method is evaluated separately on parameter response, intention fidelity and collaboration cost.

In the main case of a daily-carry aerial-photography quadcopter, the ratio of lateral to longitudinal motor span and the ratio of propeller diameter to diagonal wheelbase both changed monotonically with the input levels under a two-blade part condition image, while text carrying the same information did not. In a pilot on the open-source backend, four of the five non-baseline form levels were read as the target level in both repetitions. The formal form-response batch is expected to report the response rate of each level and the drift of non-target parts; the designer is expected to screen and revise candidates in three directions by changing levels, at most twice, leaving a complete version chain; independent raters are expected to recognise the main word-pair intentions in the final designs, and the bounded revisions are expected to reach their targets with limited generation calls and time. An agricultural spraying hexacopter case is expected to show that the layout conditions and part levels transfer to a six-rotor configuration, while payload-related parts require new levels. The study is expected to show that writing design intentions as editable form levels, with the designer in control of revision and stopping, keeps generated layouts and key part relations checkable and traceable while preserving room for design exploration. 〔Expected results; to be rewritten after the experiments.〕

Keywords: UAV form design, parametric design, controllable image generation, human–AI collaboration, design semantics, ControlNet
