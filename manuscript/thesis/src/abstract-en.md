# ABSTRACT

A quadcopter concept can place all four motors correctly yet depict a forward-facing camera as a lens on the upper shell. To locate such discrepancies, this study translates form-related statements in user reviews into part specifications. Each specification identifies the intended change and the features to retain. A configurator converts these selections into a top-view guide and a prompt.

Thirty-six evidence cards were drawn from 35 sources, and a second coder independently assessed 40 anonymized excerpts. Agreement was highest for compact versus expansive form (κ = 0.60), but references to the folded and deployed states precluded assigning this distinction to a single form parameter. In a quadcopter layout experiment guided by a diagram with two-bladed propellers, all 12 images for each of two ratios fell within the preset tolerance of ±0.10. The corresponding text-only groups yielded zero of six hits for lateral-to-longitudinal motor span and four of six for propeller diameter relative to diagonal motor span. The design workflow used a different guide showing only motor centres and arm directions. In its formal set of 24 images, two isolated AI readers each identified all four target images for each of three features: a chamfered body, hinged arm roots and a multi-panel top cover. Each reader identified a projecting nose in only two of its four target images. In the agricultural hexacopter case, overlap with the lateral arms made the spray boom difficult to distinguish from above.

In the quadcopter case, the designer retained the selected body and arm roots, removed a dark strip that obscured the apparent heading and compared the same camera housing at opposite ends. The wider end was selected because the shell partly concealed the housing. In a side-by-side comparison, all three participants judged the cover with two fewer transverse seams visually simpler. In a separate, image-by-image assessment, all three respondents rated the orange-accented version more conspicuous, but only one recommended developing it further. Assessing the requested edit separately from preservation of adjacent parts gave the designer a basis for continuing generation, reverting to an earlier version or refining the junction manually.

Keywords: UAV form design, parametric design, controllable image generation, human–AI collaboration, design semantics, ControlNet
