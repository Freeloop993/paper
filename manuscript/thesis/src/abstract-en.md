# ABSTRACT

Designers of aerial-photography drones may ask for a simpler body or a smoother transition from arm to fuselage. An image can change substantially without changing the intended part. This study investigates how to express such intentions as editable form specifications and how to decide which features to revise or retain.

Design terms drawn from user reviews informed a specification card that distinguishes target parts, permitted changes and features to retain. Two inputs served different purposes. A diagram depicting motor centres and two-blade propellers was used to test quadcopter layout ratios. A point-and-line diagram of motor centres and arm directions, together with part-specific phrases, supported the exploration of body shape, arm roots, nose and cover panels. The designer could edit a selected concept, return to an earlier version or refine a part manually.

With the tested model and component diagram, all 12 images for each of two measures fell within the specified tolerance of ±0.10: lateral-to-longitudinal motor span and propeller diameter relative to diagonal motor span. The corresponding text-only groups yielded zero and four hits out of six, respectively. For the latter measure, the text-only means stayed close to 0.48 at all three target levels. In the quadcopter design case, the designer removed a dark strip that obscured the apparent heading, compared the same camera housing at either end of the body, and chose the wider end, where the shell partly covered the housing while retaining the selected body and arm roots. In an exploratory agricultural hexacopter case, extreme layouts contracted towards the baseline and the lateral arms obscured the spray boom in top view. These findings establish the conditions of layout control and illustrate how part relationships informed selection, rollback and manual refinement. Perceived design intent and the comparative benefits of collaboration still require independent evaluation.

Keywords: UAV form design, parametric design, controllable image generation, human–AI collaboration, design semantics, ControlNet
