# ABSTRACT

Generative image models allow designers to obtain large numbers of product concept images in a short time, yet design intentions usually enter prompts as adjectives, the layout, parts and form of the outputs are hard to check item by item, and when a design is revised there is no means of telling whether the target region changed as intended and whether the remaining regions drifted. Taking the conceptual form design of multirotor UAVs as its object, this thesis proposes a human–AI collaborative method for parametric controllable generation in which design intentions are written as checkable and revisable form specifications before they enter generation.

The method has three parts. First, four bipolar word pairs, compact–expansive, simple–complex, unified–fragmented and understated–conspicuous, are derived from user reviews of consumer aerial-photography drones and mapped to features visible in an orthographic top view: body planform, arm-root transition, nose camera housing and top-cover partition. Second, rotor layout is controlled by two-dimensional condition images and form components by level-wise compiled phrases, forming a form specification card and a prototype configurator. Third, revisions start from a parent image, are limited in number and can be stopped or rolled back; each revision is checked separately for whether the target region was changed correctly and whether the non-target regions were preserved, and the point at which the designer stops generation and takes over manually is recorded.

In the main case of a daily-carry aerial-photography quadcopter, the ratio of lateral to longitudinal motor span and the ratio of propeller diameter to diagonal wheelbase both changed monotonically with the set levels under a two-blade part condition image, with read-back errors far smaller than the differences between adjacent levels, whereas text conditions carrying the same information produced no such response. In a same-seed comparison, editing from the parent image preserved the non-target regions, while regeneration from the changed levels altered the whole aircraft. The design case further shows that even when the designer retains final authority, intentions can drift when they are transcribed into prompts, and that the specification card and confirmation by restatement make such drift detectable and correctable. Preliminary results from an agricultural spraying hexacopter case indicate that layout control transfers to a six-rotor configuration, but payload components must be checked together with the layout for occlusion in the top view. The study provides a methodological basis for the checkable and traceable use of generative tools in conceptual product design.

Keywords: UAV form design, parametric design, controllable image generation, human–AI collaboration, design semantics, ControlNet
