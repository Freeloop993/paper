# ABSTRACT

Generative image tools have been applied to conceptual product design. However, design intentions usually enter the prompt as adjectives, the layout, components and configuration of the generated results are difficult to verify, and manual revisions lack traceable bounds. Taking the conceptual form design of multirotor unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) as its object, this thesis investigates a human–AI collaborative method for parametric controllable generation.

First, evidence cards are compiled from user reviews of consumer aerial-photography drones, and four bipolar word pairs, namely compact–expansive, simple–complex, unified–fragmented and understated–conspicuous, are derived and mapped to features visible in an orthographic top view: the body planform, the arm-root transition, the nose camera housing and the top-cover partition. Second, a form specification card is established to distinguish target regions, variable items, preserved items and free items; configuration parameters are expressed through two-dimensional condition images, and form parameters through phrases compiled level by level. Finally, the designer evaluates every draft, selects a parent image and revises it within a limited number of attempts, with the option to stop or roll back at any time; when a revision proves ineffective, generation is stopped and the work is completed manually, and the full version chain is retained.

The main case, a daily-carry aerial-photography quadcopter, shows that under two-blade part condition images both the ratio of lateral to longitudinal motor span and the ratio of propeller diameter to diagonal wheelbase varied monotonically with the input levels, whereas text carrying the same information did not. Text-only edits starting from the parent image preserved the non-target regions in five of six images, whereas regeneration at the new levels changed the body outline or its partitioning. The three-dimensional orientation of the camera in an orthographic top view marks a boundary of the method: none of the three revisions achieved the target, and the camera was eventually completed manually. The design case comprised three rounds of drafts and three revisions, requiring 43 calls to the generative model in total. Preliminary results from the agricultural spraying hexacopter case show that the layout constraint transfers to the six-rotor configuration, although the response contracts towards a regular hexagon, and that the specification of payload components must be checked together with the layout for occlusion in the top view. The study indicates that expressing design intentions as editable form levels, with the designer retaining control over revision and stopping, makes the layout and the principal component relations of generated results checkable and traceable.

Keywords: UAV form design, parametric design, controllable image generation, human–AI collaboration, design semantics, ControlNet
